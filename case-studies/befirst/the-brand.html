The story

Built to be first

BeFirst arrived with ambition and a clear ask: an identity that could go to market fast and age well. The brief was discipline, not decoration — "build the system first, make it impossible to dilute."

So we designed an identity that doesn't lean on ornament. A geometric "T" lockup that reads as a symbol, not a letter. A black-and-white system where contrast does the talking. Six colors with their own names — Light Grey, Stone, Blackout, Tailwind, Horizon, Oceanic — each with a job to do.

The guidelines book closes the loop: clearspace, token rules, illustration direction, typography — every decision written down so the brand stays BeFirst no matter who touches it.

Brand guidelines

The identity system, up close

No PDFs, no downloads — the whole system lives right here on the page: the lockup, both grounds, the T clearspace rule, the six named colors, typography and imagery.

The symbol

A "T" that guards its space

Clear space around the logo equals the cap height of the "T" — nothing breaks the limit that the T's create. The mark reads as a symbol first and a letter second, which is exactly what a brand awaiting its name needs.

B/W first, blue for the moments that matter. Click any chip to copy its hex.

Typography

KO Media — swiss bank sensibilities

Print and digital intertwined: strong, safe, unique. A general-use face that carries the brand's quiet confidence — no display theatrics, just authority.

Imagery

The look and feel

A visual guideline for photography: how the core branding colors and real-world images meet. Consistency over decoration.

Token imagery

One token, two dimensions

The rules: the token's 2D graphics live in the core blue or black — the 3D graphics always wear the blue gradient. Illustrations either carry the gradient or go black-and-white to keep the brand tight.

Applications

Everything working together

Examples of the logo, typography, gradients and token imagery in use together — the system proving it holds, from business card to signage.

The Challenge

Design a complete brand — logo, colors, type, imagery, applications, a full guidelines book — for a company that doesn't have a name yet. No wordmark to anchor on, no verbal identity to lean on. Every asset had to be ready for market from day one.

The Solution

Go symbol-first. A geometric "T" lockup that guards its own clearspace, a B/W-first system where contrast replaces vocabulary, six self-named colors, KO Media for quiet authority, and strict token & illustration rules. The nameplate on the cover says it plainly: BeFirst — a name that makes the promise the system was designed to keep.

Approach

Strategy

Restraint as a brand strategy. By refusing to depend on a name, the identity becomes stronger than one: the symbol carries recognition, the palette carries emotion, the typography carries trust — and the name, when it arrives, steps into a system already standing.
