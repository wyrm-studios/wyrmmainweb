The story

Why "Minato"?

Minato (港) is the authentic Japanese word for harbor — a sanctuary where journeys begin, cultures converge, and the freshest seafood arrives from the ocean. For a premium sushi restaurant sitting right on the water at Port El Kantaoui, the name isn't decoration. It's geography.

The name had to pass a hard test before it earned the sign above the door: authentic in its use of genuine Japanese language and Kanji, short enough to pronounce for locals and tourists alike, sophisticated enough to carry a premium feel — and visually strong enough that a single Kanji character could anchor the signage.

It creates a perfect bridge: Japanese tradition on one side, the maritime charm of Sousse on the other. That bridge became the entire identity — every color, every pattern, every package flows from it.

Brand guidelines

The identity system, up close

No PDFs, no downloads — the whole system lives right here on the page: the mark and its meaning, the palette, the type, and the wave pattern that ties it together.

One letter, three stories

The logo is formed from the letter "O" of MINATO — fused with a sea wave and the round silhouette of rolling sushi. Look again: it's the harbor, the ocean, and the craft, drawn as a single circle.

It holds at 32 pixels on an app icon and at three meters on a storefront — the same wave, the same roundness, the same story.

Type — clarity first

Aa 港

A clean sans-serif face, chosen because it works best when clarity is required — menus, tables, numbers and small body text. Kanji set in a serif voice for the moments that deserve ceremony.

The Challenge

Premium sushi on the Tunisian coast competes in a market full of generic takeaway branding. MINATO needed an identity sophisticated enough for a fine-dining menu, warm enough for a tourist promenade, and authentic enough to honor Japanese tradition — all while staying unmistakably local to Port El Kantaoui.

The Solution

A harbor, told visually: a name with real meaning (港 — port), a mark fusing the "O", the wave and the maki, a disciplined five-color palette ruled by slate depth and one coral accent, packaging with the wave pattern at 40% opacity, and a bilingual voice — Japanese soul, Sousse address. The website is the next dock under construction.
